Case Study

CarePath

Replacing a stack of intake paperwork with a single guided path — from booking to appointment day, without the anxiety.

RoleUX Designer & Researcher
Timeline10 weeks
ClientCarePath (concept)
PlatformResponsive web
ToolsFigma, UserTesting
Overview Brand Overview

CarePath is a patient portal for independent clinics — built to replace clipboard intake forms and confusing phone tag with one calm, guided flow from booking to check-in.

Pain Problem Statement

Patients arrived at clinics having filled the same information three times — once online, once by phone, once on a clipboard — and still got asked for it again at the desk. Front-desk staff spent the first ten minutes of every visit re-keying data instead of greeting patients.

As a result
  • New-patient appointments ran 12–15 minutes behind on average.
  • 18% of intake forms were incomplete or illegible on arrival.
  • Patients rated "ease of getting an appointment" as the clinic's weakest area.

One path, not three forms.

Booking, intake, and check-in became a single continuous flow that remembers what a patient already told us.

Solution How CarePath Fits

We redesigned intake as one linked journey: what a patient enters while booking pre-fills their intake form, and what they confirm in intake pre-fills check-in — so nothing is asked for twice.

Key pain points we aimed to solve
  • Repeated data entry — one record follows the patient through every step.
  • Clinical jargon — plain-language explanations for every form field.
  • No sense of progress — a visible path shows exactly what's left.
  • Desk-side re-keying — staff review, not retype, on arrival.
01

Strategy & Approach

Shadowing four clinics showed the real problem wasn't the forms themselves — it was that no single system remembered what a patient had already shared. The approach centered on continuity:

  • One record — booking, intake, and check-in read from the same patient profile.
  • Plain language — every clinical term gets a one-line, human explanation.
  • Visible progress — patients always know how many steps remain.
02

UI/UX Visual Workflow

The flow was designed and validated with real patients across three clinics:

  • A single progress path replacing five separate forms.
  • Auto-filled fields shown with a subtle "from your booking" tag for trust.
  • A front-desk view that surfaces only what staff need to verify, not retype.
  • Large-type, high-contrast mode for older patients, on by default over 65.
03

Accessibility & Trust

Because CarePath handles medical and payment information, trust and accessibility were designed in from the start, not layered on after:

  • WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and type-scale audit across every screen.
  • Clear data-use explanations at each point personal information is collected.
  • A "review before you send" step before any intake form submits.
  • Tested with patients aged 22 to 78 across three clinics.
DK

“Our front desk used to spend the first ten minutes of every visit typing. Now they spend it actually talking to patients.”

Dr. Anita Kulkarni

Clinic Director, CarePath pilot site

Outcome Results
  • Check-in time dropped from an average 11 minutes to under 3.
  • Incomplete intake forms fell from 18% to 4%.
  • Patient satisfaction on "ease of booking" rose from 3.1 to 4.6 out of 5.
  • Front-desk overtime during peak hours dropped by a third across pilot clinics.
Reflection What I’d take forward

Healthcare UX taught me to design for the worst day, not the average one — a patient filling this out is often anxious, in pain, or doing it for someone else. Plain language and visible progress weren't polish; they were the difference between a form getting finished and getting abandoned halfway through.
