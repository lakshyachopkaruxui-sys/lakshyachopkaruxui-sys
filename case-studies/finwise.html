Case Study

Finwise

A personal-finance app rebuilds its first five minutes — turning a confusing setup flow into the reason people come back the next day.

RoleLead UX Designer
Timeline8 weeks
ClientFinwise (concept)
PlatformiOS & Android
ToolsFigma, Maze
Overview Brand Overview

Finwise helps people budget, save, and track spending without the spreadsheet. It's built for a generation that wants their finances to feel less like homework — but the app's own sign-up flow was doing the opposite.

Pain Problem Statement

New users were dropping off before they ever saw the app's core value. Onboarding asked for bank linking, budget categories, and notification permissions all up front — three trust-heavy decisions before a single "aha" moment.

As a result
  • 41% of installs never completed account setup.
  • Users who skipped bank linking rarely returned within a week.
  • Support tickets about "what does this app actually do" outnumbered bug reports.

Show value before asking for trust.

Every step in the new flow earns the next one — nothing is asked for until the user has a reason to give it.

Solution How Finwise Fits

We split onboarding into two phases: a 40-second "preview" that shows a sample budget built from a couple of quick questions, then a "commit" phase — bank linking and notifications — introduced only after the user has seen their own numbers.

Key pain points we aimed to solve
  • Trust before data — demonstrate value with sample data first.
  • Front-loaded permissions — spread requests across natural moments.
  • Unclear value prop — lead with an outcome, not a feature list.
  • One-size onboarding — branch by goal (save, budget, or track).
01

Strategy & Approach

Research showed most drop-off happened at the bank-linking step, not because users doubted the security, but because they hadn't yet decided the app was worth the twenty seconds it takes. So the strategy centered on sequencing:

  • Preview first — a working budget, built from two questions, before any account creation.
  • Ask in context — request bank access only when the user tries to see real numbers.
  • Progress, not forms — every screen shows what the user gets, not just what's required.
02

UI/UX Visual Workflow

The new flow was prototyped and tested across four rounds of moderated usability sessions:

  • A three-question goal picker replacing an eleven-field form.
  • A live sample budget generated instantly from those answers.
  • Bank linking framed as "see your real numbers," not a security form.
  • A single home screen that doubles as the onboarding checklist.
03

Testing & Iteration

Each round of testing changed a specific assumption:

  • Round 1 — the goal picker was too abstract; we added real dollar examples.
  • Round 2 — users wanted to skip bank linking entirely; we added a manual-entry path.
  • Round 3 — notification permission felt premature; moved to after first budget edit.
  • Round 4 — validated the final flow against a 12-person unmoderated study.
RP

“The first version felt like a bank application. This one felt like it was already working for me before I'd given it anything.”

Riya Patel

Beta tester, Finwise

Outcome Results
  • Onboarding completion rose from 59% to 84% in the redesigned flow.
  • Bank-linking rate increased by 33% once moved past the preview step.
  • Day-7 retention improved by 22 percentage points among new sign-ups.
  • Support volume on "what is this app" tickets dropped by half.
Reflection What I’d take forward

The instinct to ask for everything upfront usually comes from an internal need — legal, security, analytics — not a user need. Reordering the flow around "what does the user need to believe next" rather than "what do we need to collect next" was a small mental shift with an outsized effect on the numbers.
